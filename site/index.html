Every event in the match, and the clips cut around the ones you chose.

How it works

From a match video to a folder of clips in three steps.

Get the match details

Paste a link to a match on Scoresway or WhoScored and VizorPlay downloads everything that happened, with the time of each event. Or bring your own CSV file.

Choose the moments

Add your match video: one file, or one per half. Filter by player, team, type of action, area of the pitch and more. Watch every clip and make it longer or shorter.

Save your clips

Export separate video files, or join them into one highlights video.

What you get

More than a cutter.

Look deeper at a match once your clips are made.

Precise filters

Combine player, team, action type and pitch area, and preview the result before you save anything.

Annotate your clips

Draw arrows, lines and shapes on the video, shade zones, ring or follow a player, and add text. Mark the pitch to draw an offside line, measure distances in metres or add a minimap. Freeze a frame, then export the clip with your drawings.

Code a match yourself

Code a match from its video with your own codes and tags, using the keyboard. Mark where on the pitch each event happened, or record a match live. Your coded events work with the filters and clip export, and your code panels can be saved and shared.

Shot and pass maps

Charts for a match, and how a team plays across several matches.

VizorPlay xG

How good was each chance?

Every shot gets a value from 0 to 1: the chance it is scored, from where and how it was taken. It shows on the shot map, an xG race chart and player comparisons. Data: StatsBomb.

Install

Pick your computer.

Version 1.0 is not out yet. The files below will be on the Releases page when it is. These steps are for version 1.0.

Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit)

Needs nothing else.

VizorPlay_1.0.0_x64-setup.exeComing soon
  1. Download the file from the Releases page.
  2. Close VizorPlay if it is open, then open the file. It installs for your own account, so you don't need an administrator password.
  3. If Windows says Windows protected your PC, click More info, then Run anyway. This appears because the app isn't registered with Microsoft, not because something is wrong.
  4. Open VizorPlay from the Start menu.

To remove it later: Settings, Apps, VizorPlay, Uninstall. Your projects stay on your computer.

Mac

First, find out which chip your Mac has: Apple menu, then About This Mac.

  1. Open the file you downloaded. Drag VizorPlay onto Applications, then eject the disk.
  2. Your Mac blocks the first launch because VizorPlay isn't approved by Apple. You do this once; later launches open normally.
macOS 15 (Sequoia) or newer

Open VizorPlay from Applications and close the message. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, find "VizorPlay was blocked" and click Open Anyway. Confirm with your password or Touch ID, then click Open.

macOS 14 (Sonoma) or older

In Applications, Control-click (or right-click) VizorPlay, choose Open, then click Open again.

Still blocked, or it says the app is damaged?

Open Terminal, type this line, then open VizorPlay again:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/VizorPlay.app

This doesn't get around Apple's check: you are telling your Mac to trust this one app.

Install with Homebrew instead

Slower, because it builds the app on your Mac. Get Homebrew from brew.sh, then in Terminal type these lines, pressing Enter after each:

brew tap B03GHB4L1/vizorplay
brew install vizorplay
vizorplay

The first launch needs a few minutes and an internet connection to get ready.

Intel Mac that needs Homebrew (including macOS 11)

The installer on brew.sh only works on Apple Silicon Macs. A community-made, unofficial installer, homebrew-intel, sets Homebrew up on Intel Macs with macOS 11 or newer. It isn't made by VizorPlay or Homebrew, so read its script before you run it. First open Terminal, type xcode-select --install and press Enter. Then paste this line:

/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/itsmaclol/homebrew-intel/HEAD/install.sh)"

Then follow the Homebrew steps above. On Intel, VizorPlay compiles on your Mac, which can take a long time, and VizorPlay hasn't tested it.

Remove VizorPlay by dragging it from Applications to the Bin. Your projects stay on your Mac.

Ubuntu 22.04 or newer, Debian, Linux Mint (64-bit)

Needs nothing else.

VizorPlay_1.0.0_amd64.debComing soon
  1. Download the file from the Releases page.
  2. Close VizorPlay if it is open. Double-click the file to install it, or open a terminal in your Downloads folder and type: 
    sudo apt install ./VizorPlay_1.0.0_amd64.deb
  3. Open VizorPlay from your apps menu.

Other Linux types (Fedora, Arch and so on) and ARM computers aren't supported yet. To remove it later: sudo apt remove vizorplay. Your projects stay.

Updating

Your projects are kept every time.

When a new version comes out, a message appears at the bottom of the app. Close VizorPlay before you update by hand.

Windows

Click Update and restart. VizorPlay downloads the new version, checks it is genuine, closes, updates and opens again by itself. Running a newer setup file works too.

Mac

Click Update and restart. VizorPlay downloads the new version, checks it is genuine, replaces itself and opens again. Not yet tried on a real Mac: if it fails, download the newer .dmg and drag VizorPlay onto Applications.

Linux

Click Update and restart. VizorPlay downloads the new .deb, checks it is genuine, asks for your password in the usual system window and installs it. Cancel that window and nothing changes.
